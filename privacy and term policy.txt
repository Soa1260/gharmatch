Part 1: Critical Audit — Why the Current Draft Leaves You Exposed
Missing Section 79 "Safe Harbour" Language (IT Act, 2000):
In India, platforms that host user-generated content (listings, tenant profiles, messages) are protected from liability for what users do or post only if they legally qualify as an "Intermediary" under Section 79 of the IT Act and comply with the IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021. Your current draft calls Gharmatch an "introduction service" instead of explicitly invoking intermediary status, and it lacks the mandatory statutory timelines for grievance redressal (acknowledging within 24 hours, resolving within 15 days, and responding to severe privacy/nudity/impersonation complaints within 72 hours).

The "Verification Badge" Trap (Negligent Misrepresentation):
Your app has badges for "Identity checked", "Ownership documents reviewed", and "Physically inspected". If a landlord with a "Physically inspected" or "Ownership reviewed" badge turns out to be a scammer who sublets illegally and runs away with a tenant's ₹50,000 deposit, the tenant will sue you for negligent misrepresentation. A single sentence saying "None of them is a guarantee" is too weak. You need a bulletproof disclaimer stating that checks are point-in-time, visual-only, surface-level observations, and do not verify legal title, encumbrances, or criminal backgrounds.

Zero Indemnification or Liability Cap:
If a tenant damages a flat, or a landlord harasses a tenant, and one of them drags Gharmatch into an FIR or civil suit in Ahmedabad, your current terms do not require the at-fault user to pay your legal fees (Indemnification), nor do they cap your financial liability at ₹0 (since you are on Vercel Hobby and charging nothing).

No Mediative / Dispute Resolution Architecture:
You asked for a mediative component. Right now, if a dispute happens, users have no structured path other than threatening legal action or filing police complaints naming the platform. We need a Multi-Tiered Dispute Resolution Clause:

Tier 1: Mandatory Good-Faith Cooling-Off & Direct Negotiation (7 days).

Tier 2: Platform-Assisted Neutral Facilitation (where Gharmatch can optionally provide timestamped rule-snapshots and logs to clear up misunderstandings, while legally remaining a non-binding neutral facilitator).

Tier 3: Voluntary Mediation under the Mediation Act, 2023 before any court action, with exclusive jurisdiction pinned to Ahmedabad / Gandhinagar, Gujarat.

DPDP Act 2023 Non-Compliance (Privacy Policy):
India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 requires specific disclosures: identifying yourself as the Data Fiduciary, the user as the Data Principal, explicitly stating the right to withdraw consent, the right to nominate someone in case of death/incapacity, the right to approach the Data Protection Board of India, and explicit disclosures regarding Cross-Border Data Transfers (since Vercel, Neon, Cloudflare, and Resend may process data on servers outside India).
